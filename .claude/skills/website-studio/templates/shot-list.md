# Photo audit and shot list

Run at phase 2, before any section is designed. Photography is the biggest
single driver of whether a small-business site reads as credible or cheap
(taste principle T1), and it is the longest lead-time item - so it goes early.

## 1. Audit what exists

Go through the Drive folder and the client's Instagram. For each usable photo:
subject, orientation, resolution, quality, and whether it could be a hero.

Then state plainly how many *strong* photos exist. Phone photos in good light
are strong. Dark, cluttered, flash-lit, low-resolution and heavily filtered
photos are not, however many there are.

## 2. Name the shots that would carry the site

Usually 3-5. Prioritise in this order:

1. **The hero** - the business doing its thing, in its own space, with a person
   in it. Landscape, room around the subject for text.
2. **The person** - the owner, working, not posing at the camera. This is the
   credibility shot.
3. **The place** - what a customer sees when they arrive. Reassurance shot.
4. **The work** - close, tactile detail of the actual output.
5. **The customer** - a real client, mid-service or reacting, with permission.

## 3. Write the shot list for the client

The client is shooting this on a phone. The instructions must be executable by
someone who has never thought about photography.

For each shot:
- **What:** the subject, in one sentence
- **Where:** the exact spot
- **When:** the time of day, for the light
- **How:** portrait or landscape, how far back, what to leave empty
- **Avoid:** the specific failure - overhead lights on, clutter in frame,
  flash, shooting into a window
- **Why:** which section it is for, so they understand what it has to do

Rules to give them: daylight, lights off, phone horizontal for heroes, wipe the
lens, take ten of each, do not use any filter, send originals not screenshots
and not WhatsApp-compressed copies.

## 4. Plan for what cannot be shot

- **Textures, backgrounds, atmosphere, product context:** generate with
  Higgsfield (see `resources.md`), graded to match the real photos.
- **People, premises, results:** never generate. A small business gets caught,
  and it destroys the exact credibility the photography existed to build.
- **Nothing suitable and nothing generatable:** cut the section. A page that
  does not need the photo is better than a page with a bad one.

## 5. Design against the real inventory

Only now decide which sections carry images and at what size. Never design a
section that needs a photo which does not exist and is not on the shot list.

## Template

```
SHOT 1 - Hero
What:   Isaías demonstrating a lift with a client, mid-movement
Where:  Main studio floor, from the doorway corner
When:   Morning, 9-11h, blinds open
How:    Landscape, phone horizontal, stand back ~4m,
        leave the left third empty for the headline
Avoid:  Ceiling lights on, mirrors showing the phone, other clients in frame
Why:    Full-width hero - the first thing anyone sees
```
